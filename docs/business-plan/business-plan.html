1Executive summary

DenialDesk is a secure, cloud-based platform that helps Florida physician practices submit insurance claims, catch and work denials, enforce Florida's prompt-pay and appeal deadlines, and recover revenue they are legally owed.

Practices lose money to denied, underpaid and late-paid claims. Today they work those claims by hand across payer portals, spreadsheets and email, and they typically pay an outsourced billing or revenue-cycle vendor around 8% of revenue on multi-year contracts with per-user license fees on top.

DenialDesk costs 4% of monthly revenue. That is one flat, all-inclusive fee, with no per-license charges and no long-term contract, and support in English, Spanish and Portuguese.

2The problem

For an independent physician practice, revenue depends on insurance claims being paid correctly and on time. It often isn't:

Denials are worked by hand

Staff move between payer portals, spreadsheets and email. The same denial types come back again and again, and no one fixes the root cause.

Deadlines are missed

Appeal and timely-filing windows run out silently. Once a deadline passes, that revenue is usually gone for good.

Florida law goes unenforced

Florida prompt-pay statutes (Fla. Stat. §§ 627.6131, 641.3155) require insurers and HMOs to pay on time or owe interest. Practices rarely track the clock, so they rarely collect.

No sense of what's worth chasing

Without ranking by dollar value and days left, staff work the oldest claim instead of the most valuable one.

3The solution: the DenialDesk platform

DenialDesk is a multi-tenant web application. A practice's staff sign in and the platform covers the claim's whole life, from submission through payment, denial, appeal and outcome.

CapabilityWhat it does for the practice
Practice setupLocations, providers (NPI, taxonomy, Florida license) and payers, each tagged with its legal regime: Florida insurer, Florida HMO, ERISA self-funded, Medicare or Medicare Advantage.
Claim submissionSends electronic claims (X12 837P) through a contracted clearinghouse after a timely-filing check, and captures acknowledgments (999, 277CA).
Prompt-pay enforcementStarts the Florida prompt-pay clock on receipt, sends alerts, produces late-payment interest worksheets and flags uncontestable obligations.
Remittance postingImports payer remittances (835), checks balances, and captures every denial with its standard reason codes (CARC / RARC / group code).
Denial work queuesRanks denials by dollar value and days left to the appeal deadline, so staff work the most valuable claim first.
AppealsTracks each appeal from draft to decision, with deadlines calculated by the rules engine (never guessed). Recovered amounts feed back into reporting.
Evidence and auditKeeps an immutable audit log and claim version history, and exports evidence for complaints to Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation.
ReportingStandard revenue-cycle reports on denial rate, A/R, recovery and staff workload.

What sets the technology apart

  • Florida rules engine. Every statutory deadline, interest rate and threshold is a versioned, effective-dated rule with its legal citation, with business-day and holiday calendars in Eastern time. When the law changes, we update one rule instead of rewriting code.
  • Security and compliance from day one. Single sign-on with MFA, row-level tenant isolation in the database, AES-256 encryption at rest with field-level encryption for sensitive identifiers, and full audit logging. The platform targets HIPAA, OWASP ASVS Level 2 and a SOC 2 Type I audit.
  • U.S.-only data residency. Florida law (Fla. Stat. § 408.051(3)) bans offshore storage of patient data. Production runs on Microsoft Azure in U.S. regions only.
  • No code inflation. The system never changes a CPT, ICD or HCPCS code without a recorded human approval. This protects practices from fraud-and-abuse exposure.

Where the build is today

The foundation is complete: the application shell, authentication with MFA, tenant isolation with automated tests, the audit log, the rules engine, a synthetic-data generator, and per-practice BAA tracking. MVP features are well underway: claims, the denial queue, payer catalog, remittance (835) import and posting, prompt-pay clocks with interest worksheets, and the first stage of appeals. Development uses synthetic data only and runs on a pre-production environment.

4Target market

Primary: mid-market physician practices

Independent Florida practices with around $2M in annual revenue. They are big enough to feel the 8% vendor fee (about $160K a year) but too small for an in-house revenue-cycle department.

Secondary: startup practices

New practices choosing their first billing platform. They have no contract to break, are price-sensitive, and value month-to-month terms.

Why Florida first

  • Florida has a distinctive and strict legal framework (prompt pay, offshore-data ban, patient refund rules). That creates demand for a specialist and a barrier to generic national tools.
  • Florida's large Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking communities are underserved by English-only vendors. Our multilingual support is a real reason to choose us, not a nice extra.
  • Payer mix at launch: Florida-regulated commercial insurers, Florida HMOs, Medicare Part B (First Coast) and Medicare Advantage. Florida Medicaid follows in Phase 2.

7Go-to-market strategy

Phase A: design partners (Months 1–6)

  • Sign 2–3 founding practices in South and Central Florida at the standard 4%, with priority onboarding and direct input into the roadmap.
  • Measure savings, recovered revenue and time saved, and turn them into case studies in all three languages.

Phase B: founder-led sales (Months 4–12)

  • Direct outreach to practice owners and office managers with a free savings analysis: we compare their current vendor invoice with 4% and show the $80K+ difference.
  • Target practices at contract renewal, when switching costs are lowest.
  • Bilingual sales and onboarding (English, Spanish, Portuguese) for Hispanic- and Brazilian-owned practices in Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach and Orange counties.

Phase C: repeatable channels (Year 2+)

  • Referral program: credits for practices that refer peers.
  • Partners: practice-management consultants, healthcare CPAs, medical associations and county medical societies.
  • Startup practices: partnerships with healthcare attorneys and lenders who advise physicians opening a practice.
  • Content: Florida prompt-pay and denial guides in three languages, plus webinars.

Onboarding and retention

Because we are month to month, we have to earn every renewal. Each client gets a monthly value report showing dollars recovered, interest collected, denials overturned and fees saved compared with their previous vendor.
